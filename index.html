Ancestral Cross River Tradition · Calabar Hospitality in Rivers State

Where Ancient Forest Harvests Meet Culinary Artistry

In Cross River culture, cooking is not merely preparing food; it is an intimate expression of respect, patience, and honor for guests. At Calabar Kitchen, we import our wild ukazi leaves, native uyayak pods, and aromatic peppers straight from Cross River soil directly to our kitchen at 41 & 42 Ikwerre Road.

01.

The Sacred Art of Afang

Wild Forest Vine & River Harvest

Afang is more than a dish; it is a celebration of Cross River rainforest abundance. The wild Ukazi vine is hand-shredded into hairline ribbons, pounded in a wooden mortar to release its deep earthy undertones, and cooked alongside lush waterleaf to create a glossy, nutritious tapestry crowned with river periwinkles.

02.

Afia Efere: The Pounded Yam Blessing

Aromatic White Soup Without Palm Oil

Unlike other Nigerian soups, traditional White Soup completely eschews red palm oil. Instead, it derives its velvet body from boiled white yam mashed directly into the broth, accompanied by the sweet, woodsy fragrance of roasted uyayak pods and fragrant uziza seeds.

03.

The Spice Council of Calabar Pepper Soup

Medicinal Warming Broths

Simmered with native alligator pepper, roasted calabash nutmeg, uda pods, and scent leaves, our pepper soups are traditional restorative broths made to invigorate the body and awaken the palate with honest, bold heat.
